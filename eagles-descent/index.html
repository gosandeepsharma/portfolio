APOLLO 11 LUNAR MODULE LANDING SIMULATOR
EAGLE’S DESCENT
Step into the Apollo 11 Lunar Module (LM, pronounced “lem”), and into Neil Armstrong’s shoes.
You have separated from the Command Service Module, and the Apollo guidance computer has flown the LM down from orbit. Now the surface is rising to meet you, riddled with craters and boulders.
Take the (simplified) controls during the final descent — read the terrain, control the descent rate, manage fuel, and put her down soft to recreate the historic landing.

LPD SCALE — the Landing Point Designator, a row of degree marks etched on the window glass. The computer showed an angle on the DSKY. The commander looked out through that mark on the scale and saw the exact patch of ground the computer was steering to. If that patch looked bad, he nudged the hand controller and the computer chose a new one. The target ring does the same job here.
2
YOUR SHADOW — the LM’s shadow, thrown ahead by the low sun behind you. It slides toward you as you descend and meets the craft at touchdown.
3
CRATERS — dark bowls with bright rims. The shadowed near wall shows their depth. Avoid them when choosing a site.
4
TARGET RING — the computer’s aim point. Hold SPACE and tap the arrow keys to move it. Visible during P64 only. A simplification: Armstrong read an angle on the DSKY against the window scale instead.
5
DUST — blown up by the engine below about 100 ft. It hides the surface, so fly the last seconds on the instruments.

PROG — the active guidance program. P63 and P64 are automatic. P66 is the final descent phase, flown by hand. It begins at 500 ft, or when you press P.

MASTER ALARM — the master warning light. Here it lights red on fuel exhaustion or a tilt beyond 30°, which includes the computer’s own pitch-over during braking. Press the lamp to silence and clear it. In PILOT mode the pitch-over alarm clears itself after five beeps. In COMMANDER mode you must press it. The real one answered the caution and warning system and computer alarms.
3
FUEL LOW — lights amber when less than 5.6% of descent propellant remains.
4
HORIZ VELOCITY — velocity across the surface (FPS). The vertical needle is left/right drift, the horizontal is forward/back. Land with both near zero.
5
ALT — vertical distance of the craft from the lunar surface, in feet (FT).
6
ALT RATE — rate of change of altitude (FPS). Negative values indicate descent. ROD SET is the rate commanded with W/S.
7
ATTITUDE — orientation of the craft relative to the Moon’s horizon. A centered line means the craft is upright.
8
THRUST — descent engine power as % of maximum. Only 10–60% is usable.
9
FUEL — descent propellant remaining (%). Below it, an estimate of burn time left at the current throttle (min:sec). The real cockpit had no such timer.
10
ABORT STAGE — jettisons the descent stage and fires the ascent engine back to orbit. This ends the mission (A).
11
LUNAR CONTACT — lights blue when the 5.6 ft probes below the footpads touch the surface. Stop the engine (X).
THE THREE PROGRAMS
The PROG display shows which guidance program the computer is running.
P63 BRAKING
Fully automatic. The computer cancels the craft’s orbital speed. Eagle’s braking burn ran about eight minutes. It is compressed to a few seconds here. Press ENTER to skip ahead.
P64 APPROACH
Automatic. The computer steers toward the target ring. Your job is to judge the site and move the ring to safer ground with SPACE and the arrow keys.
P66 LANDING
The final descent phase, flown by hand. Armstrong selected it himself near 500 ft. Here the computer hands you the ship at 500 ft, or earlier when you press P. Every key is live from then on.
In PILOT mode the computer also holds the descent rate you set with W and S. In COMMANDER mode you fly the throttle yourself with SHIFT and W or S.
KEYBOARD CONTROLS
A key held down keeps tilting the craft. Tap the opposite key to level off. Only P66 accepts flight inputs unless you take control early with P.

HOW TO LAND SOFT

1Read the site during P64. Dark bowls with bright rims are craters. Speckled ground is a boulder field. Pick smooth grey ground and move the target ring onto it early, while the range is long and corrections are cheap.

2Kill the drift first. At the P66 hand-off at 500 ft, null both HORIZ VELOCITY needles to under 4 ft/s before you work the descent rate. A small tilt held for a few seconds is enough. Level off with the opposite key.

3Ride the rate down. Use W and S to set the descent rate. A good profile is about 3 ft/s at 100 ft and 1 to 2 ft/s in the last 20 ft. Watch your shadow slide toward you. When it reaches the craft you are seconds from contact.

4Stop the engine at the light. When the blue LUNAR CONTACT light comes on, press X. Cut too late and the craft bounces. Run the tank dry and it falls. Keep an eye on TIME TO DRY. Houston calls sixty and thirty seconds.

THE THREE PROGRAMS

P63 and P64 fly themselves. The computer brakes, then steers toward the target ring while you judge the ground. Your control begins with P66, the final descent phase, which Armstrong selected by hand near 500 ft. See the INSTRUMENT PANEL tab for the PROG display and each program in detail.

WHAT ARMSTRONG DID

Eagle’s computer was steering into a boulder field beside West crater. Armstrong took manual control at about 500 ft, flew past it, and set down with under a minute of fuel left. The same choice is yours on every descent.

CONTROL MODE
PILOT
The computer holds attitude and works the throttle. You steer and click the descent rate — as Apollo was actually flown.
COMMANDER HARD
No descent-rate hold in P66 — you fly the throttle by hand (SHIFT+W/S) inside the engine’s 10–60% band.
REMEMBER
Tilt toward the needles to kill drift. A tilt stays until you tap the opposite key. Watch your shadow slide in. Stop the engine at the blue light.
